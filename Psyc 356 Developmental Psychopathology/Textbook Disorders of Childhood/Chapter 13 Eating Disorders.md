Goals
 1. Summarize the components and processes related to body image, body esteem, and body dissatisfaction.
	 - body image: how individuals think, feel, and behave regarding their own bodies; a central part of identity development
	 - body esteem: the degree to which one is satisfied with their physical appearance; weight and others' evaluations
	 - body dissatisfaction: a negative attitude toward one's body
		 - most children are satisfied with their bodies but preoccupation emerges in adolescence
2. Critically analyze the information related to body dissatisfaction across childhood, adolescence, and early adulthood.
	- concerns about body shape and appearance begin in elementary school and often increase through early childhood
	- demographics: low body esteem pathways are predominantly composed of girls, who typically focus on thinness while boys focus on muscularity
	- risk factors: parental modelling of dissatisfaction, family teasing, peer shaming, and media exposure; westernization and modernization idealize thinness
3. Summarize the core characteristics of anorexia nervosa.
	- core characteristics: significant restriction of energy intake leading to low body weight; intense fear of gaining weight; distorted perception of one's own body size or shape
	- subtypes: 
		- restricting type
		- binge eating/purging type
	- clinical picture: patients often deny the seriousness of their low weight and may become socially isolated or argumentative about food
4. Summarize the core characteristics of bulimia nervosa.
	- core characteristics: recurrent episodes of binge eating followed by compensatory behaviours to prevent weight gain (vomiting, laxative use, excessive exercise)
	- criteria: binging and compensation must occur at least once a week for 3 months
		- weight may remain within a relatively normal range
5. Summarize the core characteristics of binge eating disorder.
	- core characteristics: repeated episodes of binge eating accompanied by a sense of lack of control and significant distress
	- distinction: unlike bulimia, BED doesn't involve regular compensatory behaviours like purging. often more prevalent in youth who are overweight
6. Summarize the core characteristics of avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder. (ARFID)
	- core characteristics: eating disturbance based on lack of interest in food, sensory sensitivity, or fear of aversive consequences (choking)
	- results in significant weight loss, nutritional deficiency, or dependence on tube feeding
	- not motivated by body image concerns or weight fear
7. Describe several developmental pathways for youth diagnosed with eating disorders.
	- onset peaks: most common in early adolescence and late adolescence coinciding with major developmental transitions
		- explain more in text
	- course: many youth experience a fluctuating course of weight gain and loss; crossover between disorders is common
	- comorbidity: eating disorders are frequently diagnosed alongside depression, anxiety, and substance use disorders
8. Integrate the findings related to genes, heredity, physiological factors, psychological factors, parent and family factors, and environmental factors that contribute to the emergence and maintenance of eating disorders.
	- genes/heredity: both anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa show strong heritability, with genetic effects increasing across puberty
	- physiological: involves abnormal activity in brain regions related to appetite and reward (prefrontal and temporal lobes)
		- pubertal hormones and early maturation are also significant risks
	- psychological: key traits include perfectionism, negative emotionality, impulsivity, and fear of negative evaluation
	- family/environment: early models blamed "enmeshed" families, current research suggests families are not the primary cause-but family dysfunction, weight-related teasing, and social media pressure are environmental risks
9. Summarize the best practices for the assessment and diagnosis of eating disorders in youth.
	- Medical focus: a comprehensive medical exam is essential due to life-threatening complications like cardiac symptoms or anemia
	- challenges: adolescents often strongly deny their disorder, making strong therapeutic alliance critical
	- tools: diagnostic instruments include: structured interviews, self-report questionnaires, and parent reports
10. Provide a critical analysis of the data related to family-based and individual treatments for eating disorders.
	- Family-based therapy/Maudsley approach: leading treatment for adolescents; places parents in a central role to first control and restore the child's eating, later transitioning autonomy back to the adolescent
		- generally more effective for anorexia nervosa remission than individual therapy
	- CBT: a specialized version that targets cognitions and behaviours which are maintaining the disorder; often the treatment of choice for young adults
	- Inpatient care: used primarily for immediate medical and nutritional stabilization
	- Pharmacology: antidepressants may help with comorbid conditions but are generally not effective for weight restoration in anorexia nervosa

Developmental tasks and challenges related to eating and appearance
- body image: how individuals think, feel, and behave in relation to their own bodies
	- an important component of identity
- body esteem: level of satisfaction with one's body
- puberty marks a significant increase in awareness of adolescents of their body image and esteem
- components of body dissatisfaction: thinness-oriented and muscularity-oriented dissatisfaction
	- associated with unhealthy eating and weight control measures
	- predicts future long-term difficulties in well-being
- stable developmental trajectories:
	- high body esteem across late childhood - early adolescence
	- moderate body esteem across time
	- low body esteem across time
- Parent factors include intrusive concerns, pressure to manage weight, and modeling of body dissatisfaction
	- Family teasing related to weight and appearance is associated with decreased esteem and higher rates of depression
- Peer factors include teasing and shaming, peer comparisons, and peer dieting
- Media exposure “(1) normalizes dieting and excessive thinness, and (2) encourages young people to repeatedly evaluate their bodies, to find them wanting, and to engage in extreme dieting, over exercising, and other health-compromising behaviors"
- Body dissatisfaction and desire for thinness more frequently reported in high-SES settings
	- modernization and westernization bring cultural changes that promote a thin ideal

Eating disorders
- Anorexia nervosa has 2 subtypes: a restricting type and a binge eating/purging type
- disturbances in eating behaviours, perceptions of body size and shape, fear of being fat, and compensatory behaviours to lose/gain weight

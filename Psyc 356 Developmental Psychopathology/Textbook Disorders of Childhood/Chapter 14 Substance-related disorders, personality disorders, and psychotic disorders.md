Goals
1. Describe the adolescent developmental tasks and challenges related to brain development, self-regulation, and identity and personality.
	- Brain development: critical changes occur in brain structure and function, particularly in the PFC, which supports self-regulation.
		- maturation is complex and continues into early adulthood
	- Self-regulation and motivation: the dual systems model suggests a developmental mismatch between early-maturing incentive motivation systems (reward seeking) and later maturing cognitive control systems (self-regulation)
		- This can lead to increased risk-taking in high-emotion situations
	- Identity and personality: most adolescents successfully navigate identity formation, but some experience a temporary dip in personality maturation known as the disruption hypothesis
		- more in textbook
2. Summarize the core processes involved in substance use disorders, including physical dependence, withdrawal, psychological dependence, and addiction.
	- Physical dependence: occurs when the body requires the substance to function normally often involving tolerance and withdrawal symptoms
	- Psychological dependence: emotional or mental compulsion to use a substance to achieve a specific feeling or avoid discomfort
	- Addiction: chronic disorder characterized by compulsive drug seeking and use despite harmful consequences
3. Describe multiple pathways for youth diagnosed with substance use disorders and behavioral addictive disorders.
	- Gateway hypothesis: suggests a typical sequence of use, starting with a softer drug leading to harder ones
	- Common liability to addiction model: proposes a shared underlying vulnerability contributes to all types of substance use problems
	- Developmental cascades: early childhood stressors and poor inhibitory control can set off a series of negative interactions that lead to adolescent substance use
	- Behavioural addictions: problematic internet use, gaming, and gambling are increasingly recognized as involving similar processes to substance addictions
4. Explain how combinations of risk factors lead to the emergence and maintenance of substance use disorders and behavioral addictive disorders.
	- Genetics: twin and adoption studies show moderate-to-high heritability for alcohol and drug dependence, with genetic influence increasing throughout adolescence
	- Physiological: involves the incentive-sensitization theory-repeated drug use sensitizes brain reward circuits making the drug highly salient
	- Psychological: key risk traits include high sensation seeking, impulsivity, and low inhibitory control
	- Social/environmental: influences include parental monitoring, family conflict, peer use, and sociocultural attitudes towards substances
5. Summarize the important components of a comprehensive assessment for substance use disorders and behavioral addictive disorders.
	- Informants: assessments should include reports from the adolescent, parents, and teachers and peers
	- Components: must screen for the frequency and severity of use, comorbid psychopathologies (depression, ADHD) and the adolescent's underlying personality
	- Strengths-based: appraising an adolescent's personal strengths is critical for building rapport and determining readiness for change
6. Provide a critical analysis of the treatments for substance use disorders in adolescence.
	- Prevention: information programs targeting youth
	- Motivational interviewing: brief, person-centered intervention designed to strengthen the adolescent's own motivation for and commitment to change
	- Psychosocial approaches: these include individual therapies, family therapies that address the home environment, and group interventions (risk of peer contagion)
7. Provide a critical analysis of personality pathology and personality disorder in adolescence.
	- Definition: personality disorders are maladaptive patterns of experiencing the self and relationships that are associated with significant distress
	- Adolescent sensitivity: adolescence is now viewed as a sensitive period for the development of these disorders, and early identification is seen as vital for improving adult outcome
	- Models: dimensional models focus on impairments in identity, self-esteem, empathy, and intimacy
8. Summarize the important developmental processes related to the emergence of psychotic disorders in late adolescence
	- Prodrome: a period of gradual decline in functioning before the first full psychotic episode, often including symptoms like social withdrawal or unusual thought patterns
	- Neurodevelopment factors: psychotic disorders like schizophrenia may involve disrupted synaptic pruning, leading to abnormal brain connectivity
	- Etiological models: integrate genetic vulnerabilities, prenatal complications, and postnatal stressors that trigger the transition to psychosis
	- Intervention: identify youth at clinical high risk to provide early psychotherapy and education that may delay or prevent full psychosis
